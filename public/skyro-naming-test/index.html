Naming Test:
One Name for Credit

Skyro currently runs three credit products — Credit Line, POS Loans, Cash Loans. The goal is to unify them under a single UI-level name that reads as intuitive, builds trust, and fits the Skyro brand across four target markets. 36 synthetic personas evaluated four candidate names against six diagnostic questions: what is this product, what's included, is "Credit Limit" understood as a revolving line, what does "Limit" alone evoke, what associations emerge overall, and which name brings the customer fastest to "I get it — let me apply".

«Skyro Credit Limit» wins because it is the only candidate that simultaneously carries brand attribution, a market-recognized product descriptor, and a trust signal of seriousness. «Skyro Credit» is the strong runner-up — wins with Gen Z and time-poor mass-market segments — but loses on the core diagnostic: people read it as "Skyro is a lender", not as "a specific product I can apply for". «Skyro Limit» failed unanimously — the word «Limit» alone is ambiguous across all four markets (spending limit on a card vs. account limit vs. credit cap). «Credit Limit» without brand wins only with bank-trained professionals who treat it as an industry term — and loses everyone else to "which company's credit limit?".

Key findings

Nine insights that explain the verdict.

20
of 36 personas chose Skyro Credit Limit as #1. The only candidate that wins across every market and every income tier. Strongest with bank-aware professionals, Muslim mass-market, OFW remittance audience, and SME owners — i.e. the segments where decision speed matters most.
11
chose Skyro Credit as #1 — the runner-up. Concentrated in Gen Z (Brian Otieno 9.0, Content Creator 9.0), time-poor service workers (Call Center, Driver, HORECA), and Kenya's mass market. The name is faster — but at the cost of "what's actually included".
0
chose Skyro Limit. Unanimous loss across 36/36. The word "Limit" alone has no category signal. In every market it pulled associations to telco data plans, gym memberships, account ceilings — never to credit. Remove from consideration.
5
chose plain Credit Limit as #1. All five are either bank-trained (Mohammed, Government Employee, Peter Mwangi) or extreme mass-market who need the simplest possible category cue (Rural Worker, John Kamau). For everyone in between, "Whose credit limit?" creates friction.
✓
Q3 — does "Credit Limit" mean revolving line? Bank-trained personas: 100% yes. Mass-market: 55% yes, 30% confuse with one-time loan, 15% don't have a frame. The Skyro brand prefix doesn't shift this — Q3 scores are nearly identical for Credit Limit (6.8) and Skyro Credit Limit (7.2).
!
"Limit" associations cluster around restriction, not headroom. Standalone: "ceiling", "max allowed", "blocked", "you can't go further". Paired with "Credit": flips to "available room", "credit you can use". The word is only safe in a phrase, never alone — final nail in Skyro Limit's coffin.
△
"Skyro Credit" carries debt-tone risk in Muslim markets. Ahmad (MY), Siti (MY), Omar (KSA) all flagged "Credit" without softening as awkward for Shariah-conscious buyers. "Skyro Credit Limit" reads as a product name — the loaded word is buffered by the descriptor. Subtle, but it matters.
★
Skyro Credit Limit lands fastest with the OFW / remittance audience. OFW (9.5 / SCL), Nurse (8.5), Housewife (7.5) — segments that handle money for multiple people and value clarity over slang. "Brand + descriptor" reads as "a serious tool I can trust with the family's money".
→
Recommendation: Skyro Credit Limit as the product name across all four markets. Use the full form on first contact, activation, and product cards. Allow "Credit Limit" or "Limit" as short forms in in-app context ("Your Credit Limit", "Adjust Limit") where ambiguity is already resolved. Never use "Skyro Limit" as a public-facing label.

KEY INSIGHT
"Skyro Credit Limit" wins because it softens the loaded word "Credit". In a Shariah-aware market, "Credit" standing alone feels harsh. The descriptor "Limit" reframes it as a product feature, not a debt category. Mohammed (banker) explicitly preferred the full term over the brand-only forms.
CREDIT TONE WATCH
Omar (savings-oriented) chose plain "Credit Limit" — to keep Skyro at arm's length from the word. Signal: the more religiously cautious the persona, the more they want the brand decoupled from "credit". Worth testing a Shariah-compliant sub-line.
PREMIUM EXCEPTION
Khalid (entrepreneur) was the only one to pick "Skyro Credit". Reason: brand+short = premium, modern, Revolut-feel. Read this as a signal — for high-income tech-aware KSA, "Skyro Credit" could be a card sub-brand, not the umbrella name.
RECOMMENDATION
Use "Skyro Credit Limit" as the primary KSA name. Consider a parallel Shariah-compliant variant ("Skyro Murabaha" or "Skyro Tayseer") for the segment Omar represents. Premium / SME audience can see "Skyro Credit" as a card name once they're inside.

KEY INSIGHT
Kenya is the only tied market — 4/4 between full form and short form. The split is cleanly aspirational vs. survival: upmarket and women lean to "Skyro Credit Limit" (Alice 8.5, Faith 8.0), Gen Z and casual-income workers lean to "Skyro Credit" (Brian 9.0, James 7.5).
M-PESA REFLEX
Kenya thinks in payment categories, not credit ones. Boda boda driver James and market seller Mary read "Skyro Credit Limit" as "too bank-like" — they wanted something with the rhythm of M-Pesa: short, action-oriented. "Skyro Credit" fit. This is local syntax, not a verdict against the name.
PEER-TRUST CHECK
Peter Mwangi (teacher) picked "Credit Limit" alone — for the same reason he trusts SACCO over apps: brand-decoupled language reads as more institutional, less marketing-coded. Mirrors how Equity Bank labels its products.
RECOMMENDATION
Lock "Skyro Credit Limit" as the umbrella name. But: Swahili / Sheng adaptation in marketing copy can compress to "Skyro Credit" for younger urban audiences (Brian, Mary). Onboarding and product cards stay full. Kenya is the only market where ad-creative can flex the name without breaking the system.
— Final recommendation

Lock Skyro Credit Limit
as the umbrella name.

It's not the shortest name. It's not the punchiest. But it's the only one of four that wins across every market, every income tier, every age cohort — without exclusion. It carries the brand. It declares the category. It buffers the loaded word "Credit" with a descriptor that softens debt-tone in Muslim markets and sharpens product clarity for everyone else. The runner-up — Skyro Credit — has a role, but not as the umbrella.
