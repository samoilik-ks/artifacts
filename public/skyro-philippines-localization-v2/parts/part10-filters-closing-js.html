The Five Filters — Philippine Version

Before you apply the filters — here is the process every piece of localized advertising should go through:

1
Take the Insight
Start with the Overview — market context, values, brand persona. Ground your idea in local reality.
2
Build the Creative
Use Brand Voice as your compass — check the Do/Don't guides for tone and cultural guardrails.
3
Legal & Compliance
Run it past Legal. BSP and SEC requirements are non-negotiable. Religious sensitivity review if applicable.
4
Run the Five Filters
Every creative must pass all five filters below. If it fails one — rework, don't ship.
The filters below are your final quality gate. They are not a substitute for the process above — they are the last check before anything goes live. Apply them rigorously.
FILTER 01

The Golden Rule — Could GCash or Maya have written this?

If the answer is yes, rewrite it. What makes Skyro PH distinctive is not just what it offers but how it speaks: with specific cultural intelligence, genuine malasakit in the details, and a voice that treats financial communication as a conversation between equals. If a line could belong to any fintech, it does not yet belong to Skyro.

FILTER 02

The Kitchen Table — Would a sari-sari store owner in Davao and an OFW family in Cebu both understand this clearly?

If the answer is no — if the language is too urban, too formal, too Manila, too abstract — simplify. Use Taglish. Use concrete numbers. Use real situations. Financial literacy varies enormously across this market; the best Skyro communication works for everyone from the first read.

FILTER 03

The Promise Check — Does this communication imply that the user needs to change who they are?

In a market with deep hiya (shame) sensitivity, this filter matters especially. Any hint of judgment — about spending habits, debt levels, financial choices, or cultural practices — will be felt and remembered. The question to ask: if someone read this while already anxious about money, would they feel seen and supported?

FILTER 04

The Substance Test — Is there a specific, verifiable claim here?

Post-scam-era Filipino consumers are sophisticated readers of financial communication. Vague promises are actively distrusted. Every claim needs a real proof point. "₱5.2 billion disbursed to over one million Filipinos" is real. "We help Filipinos achieve their financial dreams" is not. If a piece of copy cannot pass this test, it is not ready.

FILTER 05

The Channel Fit — Does the tone match the moment?

A payday social post and a payment overdue notification are not the same moment. A BER season campaign and a loan decline message are not the same moment. Christmas content and a fraud warning are not the same moment. Before publishing anything, ask whether this piece of communication would feel appropriate at the specific time and context it will reach its audience. If it would feel wrong, it is wrong.

Closing

Skyro came to the Philippines not as a foreign fintech adapting to a local market — but as a brand that was built, from the beginning, around the reality of people whose financial lives do not fit the standard template. The Filipino market is not an edge case. It is the core case.

The local team's job is to express the brand — fluently, specifically, and with genuine care for the people it is speaking to.

Every piece of communication should be able to answer three questions:

  • Does this respect the intelligence and the culture of the person reading it?
  • Does this make financial life feel lighter and more manageable — not heavier, not more shameful?
  • Does this sound like something a real, thoughtful person would actually say to a friend?

If yes — it is Skyro.
