Advertising Restrictions — What You Cannot Do in the Philippines

Skyro operates in a regulated communications environment. The rules below come from three distinct levels of authority — and every person creating content for Skyro Philippines must know which level they are working with.

⚠️ Three levels of obligation. Know which one applies.

  • LAW — Republic Acts, SEC and BSP regulations. Violation = fines, license suspension, legal action.
  • INDUSTRY STANDARD — Ad Standards Council (ASC) Code of Ethics. Major media will not air content without ASC clearance. No legal penalties, but non-compliance blocks distribution.
  • SKYRO BRAND STANDARD — Our own rules, stricter than any regulation. Violation = brand damage and loss of the trust we are building.
RULE 01 · LAW

Financial Advertising Regulations

All advertised interest rates, fees, and charges must be accurate, current, and presented in a way that is not misleading. Any promotional rate must disclose all conditions that apply after the promotional period — including the standard rate, duration, and any fees that alter the Effective Interest Rate (EIR). Claims about "zero interest" or "no hidden fees" must be strictly verifiable against the actual loan terms.

Legal basis:

  • RA 3765 (Truth in Lending Act) — requires disclosure of true cost of credit
  • BSP Circular No. 730 (2011) — EIR disclosure requirements for banks
  • BSP Circular No. 755 (2012) — extends requirements to non-bank lending entities
  • BSP Circular No. 1160 (2022) — updated disclosure rules for all FSP marketing materials
  • RA 11765 (Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act, 2022) — FSPs bear full legal responsibility for all claims in marketing materials
  • SEC Memorandum Circular No. 19 (2019) — the key document for lending companies: requires all advertisements to display the corporate name, SEC registration number, and Certificate of Authority number
🚩 Prohibited Examples
"100% guaranteed approval" — misleading under RA 7394 Art. 50 and RA 11765
"Zero risk loan" — false representation of financial product
"Instant cash — no questions asked" — also potentially violates AML/KYC requirements
"The best loan in the Philippines" — unverifiable superlative claim
✓ What Skyro Does Instead
Name the specific rate, the specific fee, the specific condition — before any commitment
Display SEC registration number and Certificate of Authority in all consumer-facing materials (SEC MC 19 requirement)
Let the product speak — facts over claims
RULE 02 · INDUSTRY STANDARD + BRAND STANDARD

Religious Considerations

The Philippines is the third-largest Catholic country in the world (~79% Roman Catholic per PSA Census 2020). Financial advertising touching religious symbols, language, or imagery carries substantial reputational risk. Using imagery of the cross, the Santo Niño, the Black Nazarene, or Catholic icons in promotional contexts can be perceived as deeply disrespectful.

Regulatory basis: There is no Philippine law that specifically prohibits religious imagery in advertising. The restriction comes from the ASC Code of Ethics, which requires that advertisements "respect religious beliefs and be sensitive to the diverse religions, mores, culture, traditions of Filipino communities." Major media outlets will not air content without ASC clearance — making this an effective gate even without legal force.

🚩 Do Not Use
Religious imagery (cross, Santo Niño, Black Nazarene) in commercial promotional contexts
Religious references used as marketing hooks without genuine cultural grounding
Islamic themes in BARMM communications without a separate, expert-reviewed protocol
✓ Skyro's Approach
Respectful acknowledgment of religious moments as cultural events (Simbang Gabi, Holy Week, fiestas)
Tone that honors significance without co-opting symbolism
Test: would a devout Catholic feel respected, or feel their faith is being used to sell?
Muslim community note: Officially ~6.4% of the Filipino population is Muslim (PSA Census 2020), though the National Commission on Muslim Filipinos estimates 10–11% due to undercounting in census data. In BARMM, Muslims are the majority (90.9% of households) — not a minority. If Skyro develops Islamic finance products, a separate communications protocol is required. Ramadan and Eid must be treated with full understanding, not as seasonal marketing occasions.
RULE 03 · BRAND STANDARD

Political Neutrality

The Philippines has highly polarized political dynamics. Skyro must maintain strict political neutrality across all communications.

Regulatory basis: There is no Philippine law requiring political neutrality in commercial advertising. COMELEC regulations (Omnibus Election Code, Resolution No. 11086) govern political advertising by candidates, not commercial brands. The ASC Code states that advertising "should not undermine the public's regard for government." This is a Skyro brand decision — grounded in reputational risk management, not legal obligation.

🚩 Do Not Use
Endorsements of political figures or parties — direct or implied, at any level
Campaign imagery with political colors or aesthetics from recent elections
Messaging referencing government programs implying endorsement of the administration
Any politically interpretable content during campaign periods (February through May in election years — the full campaign season, not just the final weeks)
✓ Skyro's Approach
Strict political neutrality at all times
Ambassador political association = immediate review
Focus on financial empowerment, never political positioning
RULE 04 · LAW (programmatic) + INDUSTRY STANDARD

Gender and Representation

The Magna Carta of Women (RA 9710, Section 16) directs the state to formulate policies ensuring the appropriate portrayal of women in media and advertising. While this is a programmatic obligation on the state — not a direct prohibition on advertisers — it establishes the legal framework within which the Ad Standards Council (ASC) enforces its guidelines against sexist and demeaning advertising. The Consumer Act (RA 7394) separately prohibits deceptive and unfair practices that could include gender-based misrepresentation.

🚩 Avoid
Messaging implying women are less capable of financial decision-making
Positioning women primarily as household managers rather than economic agents
OFW communications ignoring women's financial agency — women make up 58% of all OFWs, and 99% of newly deployed domestic workers abroad are women (PSA 2023)
✓ Skyro's Approach
Acknowledge women's financial agency explicitly
LGBTQ+ inclusion as a default, not a statement — representation in the visual world without making it the campaign
RULE 05 · LAW (partial) + BRAND STANDARD

Sensitive Language and Terminology

Several words carry specific risks in Philippine financial communications — some regulatory, some cultural, some both:

"Utang" (debt) carries deep cultural stigma — linked to social shame (hiya) and fear (TransUnion 2023: 42% of Filipinos fear imprisonment for unpaid debt). Favor "installment," "repayment plan," or "loan schedule" in all consumer-facing communications. Level: Brand standard

"Guaranteed" and "zero risk" — constitute misleading claims under RA 7394 and RA 11765. Closely monitored by SEC and BSP. Level: Law

"Easy money" — no specific law prohibits this phrase, but it qualifies as misleading under general Consumer Act provisions (RA 7394 Art. 50) and reads as scam-adjacent to Filipino audiences targeted by informal lenders. Prohibited by Skyro brand standards regardless. Level: Brand standard + general law

"Exclusive" and "limited" — not legally prohibited when the offer is genuinely limited. However, fictitious scarcity (fake countdowns, manufactured urgency) violates RA 7394 Art. 50 and is associated with social media scams. Use only when the limitation is real and verifiable. Level: Law (if fictitious) + Brand standard (always cautious)

RULE 06 · BRAND STANDARD

No Competitor Comparison

Comparative advertising is legally permissible in the Philippines (RA 7394, Art. 111 — competitor prices used in comparison must be from the past 90 days and representative of the local market; use of competitor trademarks requires written permission). However, it carries significant cultural risk in a market where interpersonal relationships and face-saving (pakikisama) are deep values. A campaign making GCash or Maya look bad will generate backlash and invite retaliation. Skyro's competitive positioning should be achieved through differentiation, not comparison.

RULE 07 · BRAND STANDARD

No Pressure Tactics or "Easy Money" Messaging

Promoting careless borrowing, consequence-free financial decisions, or urgency-driven manipulation is brand-destructive. There is no specific Philippine law banning countdown timers or "LIMITED OFFER" typography — but fictitious urgency (a timer that resets, an offer that never actually ends) violates the Consumer Act's prohibition on deceptive practices (RA 7394 Art. 50). More importantly: starburst graphics, countdown clocks, and pressure language are the visual vocabulary of informal lenders and social media scams in the Philippines. Any resemblance to that vocabulary will cost Skyro the trust it is building.

Key Regulatory Documents for Skyro Philippines

RA 11765 — Financial Products and Services Consumer Protection Act (2022). FSPs bear full legal responsibility for all marketing claims.

RA 3765 — Truth in Lending Act. Requires disclosure of true cost of credit.

RA 7394 — Consumer Act. Prohibits deceptive, unfair, and unconscionable sales acts and practices.

SEC MC No. 19 (2019) — Disclosure requirements for advertisements of financing and lending companies. Requires corporate name, SEC registration number, and Certificate of Authority.

BSP Circulars 730 / 755 / 1160 — Truth in Lending disclosure rules for banks and non-bank entities.

ASC Code of Ethics — Industry self-regulation. Not a government body, but major media require ASC clearance before airing content.
